# Shared seams within one recursive layer

Prepare all participating direct-child rims before any child cap or parent cut is precomputed. Identify sharing by exact source vertex and edge IDs; proximity alone never authorizes welding. Canonicalize loop rotation and direction, deduplicate repeated loop records, and distinguish two region loops sharing an ordinary two-face edge from actual nonmanifold geometry. Reject absent shared edges, more than two loop owners, and invalid source triangle incidence.

In `source` mode, keep the complete current-layer source surface unchanged. In explicitly selected `smooth` mode, fit the usual loop proposals, then solve their shared positional constraints together: use the equal-confidence least-squares target along degree-two chains and lock junctions to their source coordinates. This joint target replaces independent per-loop targets before surface deformation; it is not a fallback after a failed quality audit. Report proposal disagreement and actual joint displacement. Run the existing surface-band solver and quality audit once on the complete current-layer surface. Never relax its hard geometry checks merely because an edge is shared.

Parent and child builders must consume this same immutable surface snapshot. Recompute geometric centers and perform thickness checks on that surface. Preserve source region ownership during retriangulation, including the incident region identities of every planned seam. Keep inherited parent-contact vertices and faces immutable; smooth shared-layer processing must stop when inherited surface provenance is unknown. Recursive package reloads require fresh local IDs and fresh plans. Reject reuse when source geometry or retopology settings changed.

Record shared edge/vertex counts, total edge length, neighboring source-face area, maximum proposal disagreement, and residual target disagreement. Compare the affected area with the existing micro-defect budget for diagnostics only: small area does not waive topology, thickness, Boolean, or assembly checks. Existing downstream tolerance branches retain their own conditions and reports. The shared-seam planner applies no new tolerance waiver.

Entry points are `layer_seam_planning.py`, `layer_surface_plan.py`, and `shared_seam_topology.py`. Standalone retopology calls without a validated layer plan retain their existing guards. Passing seam planning establishes consistent surface inputs, not a printable final assembly; generated backing and final export still require their ordinary validations.
